# MiroFish — Executive Summary

**Question:** I want to install automated fresh-meal vending machines in offices, malls, hospitals, metro/bus/train hubs, airports and highways, selling home-style breakfast and rice-bowl meals at ₹129. Does this have unicorn potential?

**Answer:** The product does. The distribution model doesn't. Here is the whole case in one page.

---

## The three numbers that decide it

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **₹41.57** | Cost of food + packaging in one bowl — **32% of ₹129**. This is QSR-grade. **Your price is right.** |
| **~31 meals/day** | What a machine must sell just to break even in a captive office (59 in a paid kiosk). |
| **~3 meals/day** | What India's largest vending operator actually achieves. Daalchini: ₹12 Cr revenue across ~850 machines in FY22 = **₹387 per machine per day**. |

The gap is **5–10x**, and it is not a marketing gap. It is the structural condition of vending machines in India: they get low foot traffic, low dwell time, and low repeat.

---

## The sentence that decides the company

Working backwards from a 3-year payback, here is the most you can spend building a machine:

| Meals/day | Captive office | Paid kiosk | Mall |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | ₹3.7 L | — | — |
| 50 | ₹10.2 L | ₹0.9 L | ₹3.2 L |
| 80 | ₹20.0 L | ₹10.8 L | ₹17.5 L |

Your spec — 21 hoppers, 3 cooking chambers, flash steam boiler, PID hobs, AI vision, load cells, 3 nozzles, CIP cleaning — is a **₹20–35 lakh machine at pilot**, ₹13 L at 100 units, ₹9 L at 500.

**At the throughput a captive office will actually give you, you can afford ₹4–10 lakh.** That is the whole problem, and no amount of engineering elegance fixes an arithmetic mismatch.

---

## Four specific problems in the current plan

1. **Hopper capacity contradicts your throughput target.** Your own ratio (10 kg rice → 65 people) means one 15 kg hopper maxes out at **98 meals**. A 500-meal/day machine needs **5.1 refills a day — one every 47 minutes at peak.** That is a staffed kitchen, not a vending machine.

2. **Your key patent is largely already published.** **WO2016030906A1 (PCT/IN2014/000653)** describes hoppers, auto-washing, pre-soaking, timed rice/dal cooking, steam sterilisation, and remote ordering for a future meal time. It lapsed — but a lapsed application is still prior art against you. Budget ₹8–15 L for an FTO search and 1–2 narrow claims, not ₹3 Cr for "core technology patents."

3. **Four of your seven site types fail on the arithmetic.** Malls, metros, highways and airports are where a vending machine structurally underperforms — and they are the sites that are *easiest* to get. The path of least resistance leads to the worst economics. Only **offices and hospitals** clear their fixed costs.

4. **The overnight pre-order + pre-soak model is the weakest assumption.** Asking people to choose lunch 18 hours ahead fights how Indians actually buy food. It also drags in a pre-soak subsystem (12 hours of warm standing water = bacterial incubator) and a forecast problem you cannot solve on day one. **Made-to-order in ≤3 minutes removes all of it.**

---

## What is genuinely good in your plan

- **₹129 is the right price.** Modelled bottom-up at ₹41.57 food cost, it returns a 50% contribution margin.
- **The three-chamber architecture is right** — not for holding, but for *parallel cooking*, which is what makes a 3-minute cycle possible.
- **Tadka-on-top at the point of dispensing is your best idea.** Volatile aromatics degrade within 20–40 minutes of cooking. Finishing the dish 30 seconds before serving is a real, perceptible, defensible (and potentially patentable) quality differentiator that no competitor does.
- **Your instinct to cut motor count for cost** is correct, and should be pushed much further — from 21 hoppers to **10**.

---

## The reframe that makes it a unicorn

Stop building a vending machine company. Build a **captive-density meal brand that uses machines to remove the cost and variance of the last 20 feet.**

Three changes do most of the work:

1. **Sell to the employer, not the employee.** Employer pays ₹49, employee pays ₹80, you realise ₹129. From FY 2026-27 the meal-perquisite exemption rises to **₹200/meal** — so this benefit is essentially tax-free on both sides. One signature gets you 800–3,000 customers at one address, with *contracted* volume. This is the single biggest unlock in the document.
2. **Get the capex off your balance sheet.** Franchise the machine to operators and canteen contractors, sell the ingredient kits and software, take a royalty. This is how Daalchini reached 2,000 kiosks on $6.73M — the capex sat on someone else's books.
3. **One dish, three variants.** Not a 40-SKU menu across breakfast and dinner. Narrow menus win in India (biryani, momos, chai, dosa) because they let you perfect one thing and simplify the machine that makes it.

---

## The honest unicorn answer

**As a vending machine company: no.** ₹1,000 Cr of revenue needs ~4,900 machines and ~₹540 Cr of machine capital, deployed against a utilisation assumption no Indian operator has ever demonstrated. And machine-level EBITDA is not company EBITDA — at 500 machines you reach roughly ₹66 Cr revenue and **₹0–4 Cr of company EBITDA** after overhead. You would need ₹150 Cr of cumulative funding to reach approximately break-even.

**As a captive-density food brand: yes, plausibly.** Win 30 buildings and 40,000 employees in one city. Prove 55 meals/day and 85% uptime. Cut the machine to ₹9 L. Then franchise the system into 20 cities. 7–9 years, ₹200–320 Cr raised, and a genuine unicorn candidate — because at that point you are food infrastructure, which is what every Indian food unicorn actually is.

## What to do in the next 90 days (₹43 lakh, not ₹3 crore)

| # | Action | Cost | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tadka blind test** — 30 eaters, tadka now vs tadka 2 hrs ago | ₹25 k | Does your only differentiator even exist? |
| 2 | **Manual counter** — one office, ₹129 fixed price, 8 weeks | ₹18 L | Will people eat this, repeatedly? |
| 3 | **10 Admin Head conversations** — will an employer pay ₹49? | ₹0 | Is the unlock real? |
| 4 | **FTO search** before finalising the mechanism | ₹8–15 L | Can you own any of this? |
| 5 | **Confirm ₹129 gross or net of GST** | ₹0 | Is it ₹129 or ₹122.86 of revenue? |

**If #1 fails, drop the feature and the patent. If #2 fails, stop.**

Two-thirds of the recommended spend happens *after* you have proof that people want the food. That sequencing is the whole discipline — and it is the difference between spending ₹18 lakh to learn the truth and spending ₹3 crore to build something nobody wanted.
